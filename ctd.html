Default: TY (10-year T-note) December 2026 (TYZ6), as of 28 Oct 2026. Basket: every 7- and 10-year note with 6½ to 8 years left to maturity from 1 Dec 2026 (coupons from TreasuryDirect). The 7-year notes auctioned in late October and November 2026 are not included: their coupons are not yet known. Yields: the Treasury par curve interpolated at each maturity. Betas and vol: 65-day daily changes against the 10-year par yield. These are approximations of each note’s own market yield, so replace them with quotes for trading use.

W = normals(nScen, 1)
params = ({drift: drift / 10000, vol: vol / 10000})
run = simulate(model, W, {...params, keepScenarios: true})
names = model.bonds.map(b => b.name)
ctd = model.bonds[model.ctd0]
oaDV01 = optionAdjustedDV01(model, W, params) * 1000

pSwitch = 1 - run.prob[model.ctd0]

Each point re-runs the model with today’s generic yield shifted (every bond moves by β × shift), using 2,000 scenarios. When a CTD switch is likely, the futures DV01 departs from the current CTD’s DV01 / CF, so hedge ratios based on the CTD alone over- or under-hedge.

NoteMethodology
  1. Scenarios. The generic yield follows Brownian motion with drift: Δyg = μT + σ√T · W, W ~ N(0, 1), where T is the time to delivery.
  2. Bond yields at delivery. Each deliverable moves with its beta: yTi = y0i + βi · Δyg.
  3. CTD. Clean prices PTi are computed for settlement on the delivery date; the bond with the lowest PTi / CFi is the CTD in that scenario. CTD probability = share of scenarios.
  4. Net basis fair value. In each scenario the futures price is FT = mini PTi / CFi and the basis is BTi = PTi − FT · CFi. The fair value of the net basis is the average BTi across scenarios, since a long net basis position converges to the basis at delivery.
  5. Option-adjusted DV01. The change in expected futures price E[FT] for a 1 bp shift in today’s generic yield (central difference with the same random draws), × $1,000 per point for a $100k contract.

Conversion factors use the CME formula (6% notional coupon, term rounded down to quarters or months from the first day of the delivery month). Simplifications: a single delivery date (the timing, end-of-month and wild-card options are ignored), today’s yields are used as the expected yields at delivery unless a drift is set, and the curve moves through one factor.

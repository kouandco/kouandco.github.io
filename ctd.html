NoteMethodology
  1. Scenarios. The generic yield follows Brownian motion with drift: Δyg = μT + σ√T · W, W ~ N(0, 1), where T is the time to delivery.
  2. Bond yields at delivery. Each deliverable moves with its beta: yTi = y0i + βi · Δyg.
  3. CTD. Clean prices PTi are computed for settlement on the delivery date; the bond with the lowest PTi / CFi is the CTD in that scenario. CTD probability = share of scenarios.
  4. Net basis fair value. In each scenario the futures price is FT = mini PTi / CFi and the basis is BTi = PTi − FT · CFi. The fair value of the net basis is the average BTi across scenarios, since a long net basis position converges to the basis at delivery.
  5. Option-adjusted DV01. The change in expected futures price E[FT] for a 1 bp shift in today’s generic yield (central difference with the same random draws), scaled to one contract.

Contracts. UST: CME rules and conversion factors (6% notional coupon; term rounded down to whole months for TU and FV, quarters otherwise); TU has a $200k face, the others $100k. Eurex: the published deliverable list and conversion factors, €100k face, delivery on the 8 December 2026 delivery day. Bund and OAT pay annual coupons; UST and BTP semi-annual.

Data. UST yields interpolate the Treasury par curve at each maturity, so they ignore on/off-the-run and coupon effects. Eurex yields are each bond’s Borsa Italiana reference price yield. Betas come from 65 days of daily curve changes; for OAT and BTP baskets, which have no free daily country curve, betas use the German curve shape and vol is German vol scaled by the French or Italian / German ratio of monthly yield-change vol (FRED).

Simplifications. A single delivery date (the timing, end-of-month and wild-card options are ignored), today’s yields are used as the expected yields at delivery unless a drift is set, and the curve moves through one factor.
